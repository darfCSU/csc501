## Project Management Plan

Schumann Hudson Acquisition & Technology must replace laptops for 150 employees and install one router and two to three switches at each facility across the 48 contiguous United States. Success requires standardized equipment, coordinated shipping, regional scheduling, and consistent stakeholder communication.

## Hardware Selection and Budget

The team will inventory existing equipment, network capacity, site requirements, and application dependencies. Employees will receive standardized business-class laptops to simplify security, configuration, and support. Facilities will receive business-class routers and managed switches sized to their needs. A Cisco Catalyst 8300 Series router is a candidate for larger sites requiring enterprise WAN, VPN, and security capabilities (Cisco, 2026).

The preliminary budget includes 150 laptops at $1,400 each ($210,000), routers ($300,000), switches ($150,000), and staging, shipping, and labor ($120,000). The estimated total is $780,000. A 15% contingency ($117,000) brings the budget to **$897,000**. These figures are planning estimates, not vendor quotations.

## Project Phases and Deliverables

The project will follow five phases:

1. **Assessment and planning:** Inventory equipment, document requirements, identify risks, and approve the budget, schedule, and procurement specifications.
2. **Procurement and staging:** Order equipment, track shipments, record serial numbers, apply standard configurations and security updates, and test devices before shipping.
3. **Pilot:** Test laptop enrollment, connectivity, authentication, applications, and backend dependencies at one representative facility. Resolve problems before proceeding.
4. **Regional deployment:** Install equipment in scheduled waves. Local technicians, or qualified “smart hands,” will follow installation instructions while remote engineers validate configurations and connectivity. Each site needs a contact, access arrangements, and escalation procedures.
5. **Acceptance and transition:** Verify functionality, resolve defects, update asset records and network diagrams, obtain stakeholder approval, and transfer support to operations.

These phases are interdependent: procurement requires approved specifications, staging requires delivered equipment, and nationwide deployment depends on successful pilot results. Defined milestones and responsibilities help control project execution (Harned, 2019).

## Risks and Stakeholder Management

Risks include shipping delays, damaged equipment, incompatible configurations, inaccessible sites, and network outages. Mitigation includes shipment tracking, spare equipment, tested configurations, verified backups, rollback procedures, and remote engineering support. Failed installations will be corrected or rolled back when feasible to minimize disruption.

The stakeholders will receive an initial briefing and weekly reports covering progress, costs, upcoming milestones, risks, and decisions needed. Documented change requests will explain impacts on budget, schedule, security, and availability. Clear communication and defined responsibilities help prevent misunderstandings (Creel, 2019).
