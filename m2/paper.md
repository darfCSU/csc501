# The Unhappy Stakeholder: Nationwide Hardware Refresh Project

Schumann Hudson Acquisition & Technology

## Project Management Plan

Schumann Hudson Acquisition & Technology needs to refresh laptops and network equipment for 150 employees distributed across the 48 contiguous United States. The project includes replacing employee laptops and installing one router and two to three switches at each facility. Because the organization operates across multiple states and time zones, the project requires careful scheduling, standardized equipment, coordinated shipping, and reliable on-site support. The project manager must also address the unhappy stakeholder by communicating decisions, explaining trade-offs, and reporting progress consistently.

## Hardware Selection and Budget

The project will begin with an inventory of existing equipment, site requirements, network capacity, and application dependencies. Each employee will receive a business-class laptop with sufficient memory, storage, processing power, and security features to support daily work. Standardizing laptop models will simplify configuration, troubleshooting, warranty service, and future replacements.

Each site will receive an appropriately sized business-class router and two or three managed switches, depending on its employee count and port requirements. A Cisco Catalyst 8300 Series Edge Platform is a potential router for larger or more demanding branches because it supports enterprise WAN connectivity, VPN, security options, and software-defined WAN capabilities. Smaller sites may require less expensive models. The final selection will depend on bandwidth, security, redundancy, and lifecycle requirements (Cisco, 2026). Managed switches will support centralized configuration, network segmentation, and troubleshooting.

The project must also account for backend infrastructure. Before deployment, the technical team will verify that existing servers, storage, identity and authentication services, databases, backups, monitoring, and WAN connections can support the refreshed environment. Equipment replacement alone will not resolve performance problems caused by undersized backend systems or incompatible configurations. The team will test these dependencies during the pilot phase.

For preliminary planning, the budget assumes 150 laptops at an average of $1,400 each ($210,000), routers totaling $300,000, switches totaling $150,000, and $120,000 for staging, shipping, and on-site labor. These estimates total $780,000. A 15% contingency adds $117,000, producing a preliminary project budget of $897,000. These figures are planning assumptions, not vendor quotations. Final costs will depend on the number of facilities, equipment specifications, taxes, support agreements, shipping distances, and site conditions. Major server or storage upgrades are excluded until the infrastructure assessment identifies a need.

## Project Phases and Deliverables

The project will proceed in five phases. First, the assessment and planning phase will document employee assignments, facility locations, current equipment, technical requirements, budget, schedule, and risks. Its deliverables will include an approved project plan, equipment inventory, site list, and procurement specifications. Establishing clear scope and responsibilities early helps prevent misunderstandings later (Creel, 2019).

Second, procurement and staging will involve ordering equipment, tracking shipments, recording serial numbers, applying standard configurations, installing security updates, and testing equipment before shipment. The deliverables will include staged equipment, configuration records, shipping schedules, and site-specific installation instructions.

Third, the team will conduct a pilot at a representative facility. Technicians will test laptop enrollment, network connectivity, authentication, application access, backend performance, backups, and recovery procedures. The project manager will use pilot results to correct problems before authorizing the broader rollout.

Fourth, deployment will occur in regional waves. Local on-site technicians, or qualified “smart hands,” will unpack and install equipment, connect cables, follow documented procedures, and verify physical connectivity. Remote network engineers will apply or validate configurations and conduct end-to-end testing. Smart-hands personnel must receive clear instructions, site contacts, access arrangements, and escalation procedures before each visit. The final phase will verify acceptance criteria, resolve outstanding defects, update asset records and network diagrams, obtain stakeholder sign-off, and transfer support responsibilities to operations.

The phases are interdependent. Procurement depends on approved specifications; staging depends on receiving equipment; the pilot depends on completed staging; and the national rollout depends on successful pilot results. A phased plan with defined milestones and responsibilities helps the project manager coordinate these dependencies (Harned, 2019).

## Risks and Stakeholder Management

The distributed rollout introduces risks such as delayed or damaged shipments, incompatible configurations, inaccessible facilities, missing cables, scheduling conflicts, and unexpected network outages. Backend authentication, database connectivity, or capacity problems could also prevent users from working even when the new equipment functions correctly.

Mitigation measures will include shipment tracking, spare equipment, predeployment checklists, tested configurations, verified backups, documented rollback procedures, and remote engineering coverage during installations. Each site will have an assigned contact and a confirmed installation window. If an installation fails acceptance testing, the team will restore the previous configuration when feasible and reschedule remediation rather than leave employees without reliable service.

The unhappy stakeholder should receive an early briefing explaining the project scope, budget assumptions, rollout sequence, and operational risks. Weekly status reports will summarize completed work, upcoming milestones, budget changes, unresolved issues, and decisions requiring approval. The project manager will document change requests and explain their effects on cost, schedule, security, and service availability. This approach gives stakeholders visibility and a clear process for raising concerns without allowing uncontrolled scope changes to derail the project.

## Conclusion

A successful nationwide hardware refresh requires more than purchasing laptops and network devices. Schumann Hudson Acquisition & Technology must coordinate procurement, standardized configurations, backend compatibility, regional deployment, and on-site support. A phased plan, realistic budget, contingency funding, and consistent stakeholder communication will reduce risk and improve the likelihood of completing the project within approved constraints.
