# Making a Choice: Evaluating Three Project Opportunities

Choosing a project requires more than comparing revenue with initial investment. A project manager must consider schedule, resources, stakeholders, costs, and execution risks. Project integration management coordinates these competing demands throughout a project's life cycle (Tristancho, 2023). Based on the available information, I recommend the school district project.

## Payback Period Comparison

The school district project has the shortest estimated payback. Its $1 million investment would generate approximately $50K monthly after the first year. Recovering the investment requires 20 months of revenue, producing an estimated total payback period of **32 months**.

The municipal area network (MAN) requires $10M. The city retains all revenue during the first year and half during the second. Assuming $500K monthly in year two, the company receives $250K monthly, or $3M that year. The remaining $7M takes another 14 months at $500K monthly. Its estimated payback is **38 months**.

The housing project has the longest estimated payback. Selling 12 homes for approximately $450K each would generate $5.4M, leaving $4.6M to recover. The multifamily complex is expected to generate $70K monthly, or $840K annually, after the second year. Recovering the remaining investment would take about 5.5 years; adding the two-year development and occupancy period produces an estimated payback of **7.5 years**.

These are gross-revenue estimates, not net cash-flow calculations, because operating expenses, taxes, and other costs are unspecified. The housing estimate assumes home-sale proceeds are available when rental income begins. Expenses or delays could extend all payback periods.

## Project Evaluation and Recommendation

The school project requires integration of hardware procurement, network installation, testing, and deployment. Administrators, teachers, and IT personnel must agree on requirements and responsibilities. Jenkin et al. (2019) explain that stakeholder mutual understanding supports project success and is influenced by engagement timing. Involving district representatives and technical staff early can clarify system requirements, training, and ongoing support.

The MAN project benefits from favorable legislation and potential recurring revenue, but its $10M investment and delayed full revenue create greater financial exposure. Its revenue-sharing arrangement makes the city a critical stakeholder. Responsibilities, revenue calculations, milestones, and expectations should be agreed upon before work begins. Despite its long-term potential, its financial and technical risks make it less attractive than the school project.

Housing offers both home-sale proceeds and rental income, but creates the greatest integration challenge. Zoning, approvals, construction, financing, sales, and occupancy are interdependent; delays in one activity could affect the entire schedule. Financing adds risk: borrowing $10M at 10% would create an initial interest expense that exceeds the projected $840K annual rental revenue, before operating expenses. A partner could reduce borrowing needs but would share ownership and returns.

Tristancho (2023) identifies scope, schedule, cost, resources, stakeholders, and risk as important integration considerations. The school project offers the best balance: its smaller investment limits financial exposure. The equipment and service requirements provide a manageable planning basis. Its main drawback is that service-delivery costs and contract duration are unspecified, so recurring revenue does not guarantee strong profit. A project charter, integrated management plan, schedule and cost baselines, and formal change control would align delivery with district expectations.

## Conclusion

The school district project has the shortest estimated gross-revenue payback at 32 months; housing has the longest at approximately 7.5 years. I recommend the school project because it requires the least capital, offers the quickest recovery, and could provide continuing service revenue. Its technical, scheduling, and stakeholder risks appear more manageable than the financial exposure and coordination demands of the alternatives.
