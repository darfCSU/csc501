
Darfcloud has funding to expand biometric authentication across four metropolitan areas and 12,000+ employees. Risk management needs to be a priority. Biometrics improve security, but mistakes get expensive fast, especially when your biggest power user used to be your mom.

A major risk is a biometric data breach—and a lawsuit from Frank Azar, the strong arm of the law. Unlike a password, a stolen fingerprint or facial pattern can't simply be changed. It a modarate likelihood risk with a severe impact. Encryption, strict access controls, secure storage, independent testing, and limited data collection can reduce the risk.

We need plans for cyberattacks, network outages, failed deployments, and employees being locked out. This isn't a laptop in a basement anymore. I'd require tested backups, redundant infrastructure, recovery procedures, and fallback authentication. You don't want 3,000 people outside a building because a server had a bad day—or took an errant hockey puck.

I'd have zero tolerance for unauthorized access or major privacy violations. Scheduled maintenance and minor issues that don't affect security or operations are acceptable. Each location should run a pilot, with clear criteria for stopping the rollout if serious problems arise.

Darfcloud needs developers, security specialists, network engineers, privacy and legal experts, operations staff, and local representatives. Knowing the software doesn't make someone an expert in biometric privacy or large-scale deployment. People need clear responsibilities and proper training.

The biggest blind spots may be employee concerns, accessibility, privacy laws, and differences in local infrastructure. Employees need to know what data we collect, how we protect it, and what alternatives are available. The massive hoard of new hires at Darfcloud must be able to report problems without leadership brushing them off because the project is already approved.

I'd roll out the system in stages, learn from each location, and fix problems before expanding. We can't eliminate every risk, but we can prepare for foreseeable failures instead of acting surprised when they happen.  Adding 11,999 employees will be challenging but if those venture capitalists pay me well I'd make it happen.
