For my Darfcloud project, I would consider the project complete when the new website, [www.darfcloud.com](https://www.darfcloud.com), is running reliably and meets its intended requirements. That means users can register, sign in, and use the available features (aka play the card game and sing karaoke) without crashes or unresolved bugs that interfere with normal operation. Before closing the project, I would conduct final testing, address outstanding problems, and obtain acceptance from the stakeholders (fancy word for website visitors). It should look awesome on both desktops and cell phones, since most people are too busy to sit at a computer.

Technically, I would want to verify that the Django application and MySQL database remain stable under normal use and that data is saved and retrieved correctly. Authentication and account permissions must work as expected, and users should not be able to access features or information they are not authorized to use. I would test the Docker deployment, database backups, and recovery procedures so that a container restart or unexpected failure does not result in lost data or prolonged downtime. 

Performance should be reasonable, and application logs should be free of recurring errors that indicate underlying problems. This thing has to be reliable enough that a dude doing grad school in his 40s does not have to invest time in it. From a project management perspective, this is where I would apply the acceptance criteria and verify that the deliverables meet the defined scope before signoff.

I would put a lot of weight on feedback from actual users. I want people to find Darfcloud useful, enjoy using it, and come back because it offers something they want. User acceptance should be more than a formal signoff; continued use is a good indication that the project delivered value. Once the site is stable, stakeholders are satisfied, and operational responsibilities are transferred, I would document the lessons learned and formally close the project. A successful launch is great, but having users who genuinely like the product and make it part of their routine is what would make me consider the project a success. And an A in the class.
