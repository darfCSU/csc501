## Project Closure and Knowledge Transfer

In my semi-fictitious scenario, I am transferring from the Darfcloud web infrastructure team to the backend database team to develop a tool that organizes family photos using geographical metadata and displays them on a map. Darfcloud.com was successful, but client acceptance does not eliminate the need for a proper handoff.

I would first fill the gaps in the knowledge management database and work breakdown structure. Documentation should cover system architecture, dependencies, deployment, backups, recovery, and known issues. Another team (or my daughter Zelda when she inherits it) needs to be able to maintain the software without relying on its original developers (me).

The client's expectation of two years of continued support requires a separate agreement. I would work with management and the client to define maintenance responsibilities, response times, and how requests for new features will be handled. Fixing defects is different from developing new functionality, and those expectations need to be clear. 

Before closing the project, I would arrange a technical walkthrough with the receiving support team. Once the client accepts the deliverables and that team accepts responsibility, I would obtain formal project closure approval.

This lets management move us to the photo-mapping project without abandoning the previous client. My responsibility is to leave behind a working, documented product and a clear support arrangement, not to keep the original development team attached to it indefinitely.
